Lilo
Four arguments, four sources
Everything here comes from Family, Luma, Wise or Grok. Where an idea has no source in those four, it is not here.
1 One page, not five tabs
One real trip, today
OverviewTravel2Stay1Eat6Experience0
Nine items, five rooms. One is empty and you only find out by going there.
Tabs earn their place when each section is deep enough to get lost in. A week away is a short list in date order that four people need to agree on. The trip becomes one scroll; the plan becomes days.
From Luma
An event page holds cover, time, place, host, guests and description on a single scroll. Nothing is behind a tab, so nothing is hidden by being empty.
2 One input, not a category menu
Adding a hotel
now→pick Stay→form→name, dates, address, ref
proposed→paste→Lilo reads it→confirm
You already do the hard half: ParsedBooking carries dates, times, locations, operator, service number and reference. Make the parser the front door and let the same field answer questions too.
From Grok
One surface that both adds and answers. A visible reading step rather than a spinner, answers as plain prose rather than bubbles, and the mode pill living inside the composer.
3 State, not avatars
Four people going away is a convergence problem: flights booked, room paid, bag packed. Lilo stores all of it and shows a row of faces. Make it a grid you can read in one look.
From Family
A wallet opens on a number and a plain list. No cards, no chrome — icon, name, sub-line, value. The screen answers its own question before you touch anything.
4 Nobody has settled up
You’re owed 212.50
from four costs, itemised £2,344.00
A trip for four costs thousands, one person fronts most of it, and the reckoning happens weeks later in a group chat. Lilo already models parties with split bookings and none of the arithmetic.
From Wise
Never a total without the rows that built it. Fees itemised, a dashed rule, and the number you actually receive set biggest on the screen.
5 Dark, because the photographs are the point
Every travel app is white. On black a cover photo stops being decoration and becomes the light source — and a countdown set at 84px needs nothing else on the screen to work. Family is pure black with white numerals and a plain list; Luma tints an event page with colour drawn out of its own cover art. Both land better here than the white ground I started with, and neither is a style choice so much as a way of letting one thing dominate.
What it costs
A light mode still has to exist, and the accents need re-deriving against white.
What it buys
Covers, maps and the lilo all read brighter, and the app stops looking like every other trip planner.
Taken directly
Family — black ground, the balance as hero, label/value rows, icon-only tab bar, circular add button, white pill to confirm.
Luma — cover-first creation, ambient tint pulled from the cover, rows edited in place, a picker that opens over its own row.
Wise — the number that matters set huge, itemised rows above a dashed total, an all-caps sheet title.
Grok — the Add/Ask toggle, the reading step, prose answers with an action row beneath, controls inside the composer.
