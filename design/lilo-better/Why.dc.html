Lilo
Three arguments
Everything else in this canvas follows from these. If you disagree with the arguments, the screens are wrong — not the other way round.
1 Five tabs, one page of content
Today — a real trip

Experience0
Nine items, five rooms. Two of those rooms hold a single card; one holds nothing at all, and you only find out by going there.
Tabs earn their place when each section is deep enough to get lost in — a mail client, a store. A week away is not that. It is a short list of things, in date order, that four people need to agree on.
So: one page. Sections stack, the plan is a list of days, and nothing hides behind a tap.
Luma, Meetup, Posh and Partiful all hold a whole event on one scroll. None of them tab.
2 Stop asking what kind of thing it is

paste→Lilo reads it→confirm
You already do the hard half. ParsedBooking carries dates, times, locations, operator, service number and reference — and ImportBookingSheet shows two of those fields before throwing the rest away.
Make the parser the front door. One field takes a pasted confirmation, a link, or a sentence, and proposes what it thinks you meant.
ChatGPT’s composer as the shape: one input, attachments as chips, suggestions when it is empty.
3 The group is the product
What the app knows vs shows
knows who is a member
shows a row of avatars
never asks has everyone actually booked?
Four people going away together is a convergence problem. Flights booked, room paid, bag packed, in or out for Thursday. Lilo stores all of it and surfaces none of it as group state.
So: a who’s-in grid that answers it at a glance, decisions asked as decisions, and the live count sitting in the dock where it cannot be missed.
Partiful puts “going” in the middle of the toolbar. GroupMe asks “I’m in” / “Can’t go” and nothing else.
4 Nobody has settled up
A trip for four costs a few thousand pounds, one person fronts most of it, and the reckoning happens weeks later in a group chat. Lilo already models parties with split bookings — it has the shape of the problem and none of the arithmetic.
So: a money screen that opens with the only number you care about — what you are owed, or what you owe — and shows its working underneath.
The Wise move
Wise never shows a total without showing what built it. Every fee is a row, the total sits under a dashed rule, and the number you actually receive is the biggest thing on screen.
Split costs deserve exactly that treatment. Trust in a number is built by showing the rows.
Borrowed from
Luma — cover-first creation, rows you edit in place, and a picker that opens over the row it belongs to.
Wise — the number that matters set huge, and never a total without its rows.
Family — restraint. The countdown is the whole hero; lists lose their cards and keep a hairline.
Grok — one surface that both adds and answers, a visible reading step, and replies as plain prose rather than bubbles.
Partiful — live group state sitting inside the toolbar.
GroupMe — a decision is two buttons and nothing else.
